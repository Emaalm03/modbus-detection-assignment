# Attack 5A: Multiple Short TCP Connections (Rule 100015)

This alert is related to repeated short TCP connections from a non-authorized source to the PLC. Rule 100015 detects connections from a source other than the authorized master, `192.168.56.3`.

## Step 1: Validate the alert

The analyst should check the rule ID, alert level, source and destination addresses, connection duration, and packet counts. In this case, the alert was generated by Rule `100015` at level `5`. One example shows a connection lasting approximately `0.114` seconds, with `6` origin packets and `4` response packets. This confirms that the alert concerns a short connection, but one alert alone does not establish that a connection flood occurred.

## Step 2: Identify the source

The analyst should check `data.conn_id.orig_h` and `data.conn_id.resp_h` to identify the source and destination. In this case, the source is `192.168.56.6` (attacker VM), and the destination is `192.168.56.4` (PLC), using TCP port `502`. Since the source is not the authorized master, the connection is suspicious and requires further investigation.

## Step 3: Examine the connection pattern

The analyst should examine `conn.log` for other connections from the same source to the PLC. The records should be compared using their timestamps, source ports, connection identifiers, durations, and packet counts. In this scenario, multiple short connections from `192.168.56.6` were observed, with different source ports and similar packet counts. This pattern is consistent with repeated connection attempts. However, Rule 100015 alerts on individual connections and does not itself measure the total connection rate over a time window.

## Step 4: Correlate with Modbus traffic

The analyst should use the connection identifier, when available, to find related records in `modbus.log` and inspect the associated PCAP. This helps determine whether Modbus requests were exchanged over the connections and which functions were used. The analyst should also compare the observed activity with the normal baseline to identify any unusual communication pattern.

## Step 5: Check whether the activity was authorized

The analyst should verify whether the source was involved in an approved test, maintenance operation, or other legitimate activity. If the source is unauthorized and many short connections occur within a limited period, the activity becomes more suspicious. The analyst should also check whether the PLC or simulated process shows any unexpected behaviour.

## Conclusion

The activity is suspicious because multiple short TCP connections were observed from the attacker VM to the PLC. Rule 100015 identifies the individual connections, but it does not directly detect a connection-rate threshold. The analyst should examine the surrounding connection records, correlate them with Modbus traffic, and verify whether the activity was authorized before concluding that a connection flood occurred.
